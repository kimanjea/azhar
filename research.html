Research

Human-Centered AI for training environments.

I study how AI and XR can make people better at learning and training, from military trauma simulation to the classroom. My work spans interoperable simulation, on-device language models, computer vision for XR, and equitable computing education.

Current research and development

Six projects across industry and academia.

  • June 2024 – May 2026Exonicus Inc., Seattle, WA

    TCCC SBIR Phase II and IIE

    Lead Interoperability Researcher · Principal Investigator: Dr. Kyle Couperus

    I led research on a real-time XR medical simulation system, improving data interoperability and performance on large datasets. The work introduced new approaches to real-time medical data transmission that reduced latency and made the system more reliable, improving simulation results for military training. It meant designing data-oriented architectures, testing rigorously, and working closely with medical experts so the simulations stay accurate.

    • Leading an 18-person interoperability engineering team integrating HLA and JETS architecture for trauma simulation.
    • Driving cross-company collaboration in a five-company Simulation Interoperability Working Group cohort.
    • Commercializing live, virtual and constructive (LVC) training in six countries.
    • Partnering with US Army DEVCOM on XR usability studies for MilExTS.
    • XR simulation
    • Interoperability
    • HLA
    • JETS
    • Real-time data

  • June 2025 – September 2025AVELA – A Vision for Engineering Literacy and Access, Seattle, WA

    CourseSLM

    Principal Investigator

    I led research on training frameworks for educational language models built around data sovereignty. With a team of 3 PhD candidates and 6 interns, I fine-tuned hardware-constrained large language models using Apple’s MLX framework, so educational AI can run locally instead of sending student data to the cloud. We delivered a standalone data-activism lesson plan, backed by a specialized model, to support STEM course progression in partnership with Apple CEI.

    • On-device AI
    • Apple MLX
    • Fine-tuning
    • Data sovereignty
    • Computing education

  • January 2025 – August 2025AVELA, Seattle, WA · with Bellevue XR Labs

    Gaussian Splats for Storytelling

    Principal Investigator

    I architected a workflow that uses Gaussian splats for storytelling in XR, applying deep-learning training to 360° drone video to create true-to-depth 3D environments. With Bellevue XR Labs, I published the resources and files under the Washington Open Common Education License.

    • Gaussian splatting
    • Deep learning
    • Drone capture
    • Open education

  • July 2024 – presentISIA Lab, Université de Mons, Hainaut, Belgium

    Avatar Controller Toolkit (XR ACT)

    Research Contributor · Principal Investigator: Dr. Kevin El Haddad

    I’m contributing to a toolkit that visualizes and animates facial expressions in XR, part of the lab’s research in computer vision and human-computer interaction. The work applies both engineering and research methods to make real-time facial landmark detection more accurate and efficient.

    • Computer vision
    • HCI
    • Facial animation
    • Avatars

  • September 2024 – presentMorphic Lab, University of Washington, Tacoma, WA

    BioDepot Workflow Builder (Bwb)

    Research Contributor · Principal Investigator: Dr. Ka Yee Yeung

    Bwb makes gene research reproducible for people who don’t program. It auto-installs workflow components and offers a simple GUI for changing key parameters and adding your own data files. Because interactive visualization is central to many analyses, Bwb supports Jupyter notebooks, Cytoscape, and other tools with their own graphics.

    • Reproducibility
    • Bioinformatics
    • GUI design
    • Jupyter

  • September 2023 – presentLCI Lab, University of Washington Information School, Seattle, WA

    Wordplay

    Research Contributor · Principal Investigator: Dr. Amy J. Ko

    Wordplay is an accessible, multilingual programming platform for creating interactive typography that celebrates global diversity. Built for diverse secondary classrooms, it centers inclusion across abilities, languages, cultures and socioeconomic backgrounds, and aims to dismantle hierarchies in computing through equitable programming practice, research, teaching and community collaboration.

    • Computing education
    • Accessibility
    • Multilingual
    • Justice-centered design

Research interests

The questions behind the work.

Interoperable simulation

How XR training systems from different vendors can share data in real time, and what standards make that possible.

On-device AI

Small, fine-tuned models that run where the learner is, keeping data private and sovereign.

Computer vision for XR

Gaussian splats, facial landmark detection, and true-to-depth capture of real places and people.

Equitable computing education

Accessible, multilingual tools, and how students actually use AI when they learn.
